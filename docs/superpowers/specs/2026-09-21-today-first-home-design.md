# Today-first Home design

## Intent and scope

Make Home answer: “What is on today, and what is coming this week from the TV shows I follow?” Showtime is primarily a mobile-first responsive website: it should feel app-like in a phone browser without installation and work comfortably on a PC. The approved top-left phone mockup is the main layout reference on both widths. This milestone changes Home and its schedule metadata flow only. Profile and Recent Activity improvements, Search enhancements, a separate Upcoming screen, account authentication, sync storage, and watched controls are outside its scope.

The canonical set of followed shows is TV entries in the existing Watchlist. Saved movies remain in the Home Watchlist strip. A TV show with viewing progress but absent from Watchlist does not enter the release schedule. Opening a detail page never marks an episode watched. Watched status does not remove a dated release.

## Home layout

On phones, use one column in this order: compact branded header and Search access; Today; This Week; Coming Soon; Your Watchlist; then compact previews of existing Continue Watching, Recently Viewed, Watched Movies, and optional Trending. Today starts within the first viewport at 360px and 390px. Use current branding and design tokens, compact poster rows, readable text, accessible touch targets, and existing four tabs. No broadcast time, fictional content, hero banner, desktop sidebar, or new screen.

Today lists all saved-show episodes whose original air date equals the device-local calendar date, for the entire day. Rows show poster, show title, season/episode number, episode title when supplied, and a Today badge. Tapping a row opens `/tv/[id]`. Multiple episodes of the same show on one date form one expandable row with a count; expansion reveals each episode. The row's navigation and expansion controls must be separate accessible targets.

This Week lists dates strictly after today through the current Sunday. Group chronologically under friendly day headings, including “Tomorrow · Tuesday” when applicable. It never repeats Today. Hide the empty remaining-week section on Sunday; do not imply that a full week has no releases when Today has entries. Home shows a manageable preview; an accessible in-place expansion reveals the rest. Coming Soon lists the next known releases after Sunday in date order, with a compact preview and expansion. Season-one episode one may be labelled a series premiere; episode one of a later season may be labelled a season premiere only when that episode has its own date. Never infer an episode-one date from a season-only date. No other “returning” claim is inferred. “See all” appears only for a real destination or expansion. Full dates use DD/MM/YYYY.

Your Watchlist is a small poster strip with existing Watchlist navigation and both TV and movie entries. Desktop adapts the same mockup hierarchy in a centered maximum-width area, with balanced columns only where useful; it is not a separate dashboard. No horizontal page overflow.

## Data contract and coverage

The existing `showtime.tv-schedule.v1` cache is written only after a TV detail visit and holds one next episode plus the newest season. `getUpcomingEpisodes` selects one unwatched entry per show. It cannot supply this Home design.

Add `GET /schedule/tv/:id` to the shared TMDB API, with local/UAT and production adapters. It returns safe title/poster metadata, dated regular-season episodes, and per-season coverage: each regular season is `checked`, `unavailable` (temporary failure), or `limited` (not fully returned because of a safety cap). The whole response is `complete` only when every eligible regular season was checked without truncation. It fetches the TV summary and at most 12 newest regular seasons with positive episode counts, using at most three concurrent season requests. Exclude specials and malformed or undated episodes. Keep dates from two days before the server's UTC date onward so users at the edges of time zones do not lose their local Today. Deduplicate by TMDB episode ID, then season/episode identity; sort by calendar date and episode. Return at most the earliest 200 dated episodes and identify every season affected by that cap. Include a separately identified valid `next_episode_to_air` candidate if its season is not checked; this candidate never makes coverage complete. Keep the TMDB token server-side. Missing or malformed TV summary data returns an error, not a confident empty list. The response does not assert UK availability.

Production keeps the existing origin restrictions, rate limits and work-cost reservation. Complete and cap-limited 200 responses may use the existing ten-minute server cache. A 200 response with temporarily unavailable seasons is not server-cached, so a retry can recheck those seasons. 429 and 5xx responses are not cached. A manual Retry bypasses client freshness but still respects server rate limits and any valid complete/cap-limited server cache.

The client validates the response and stores it in the existing device-local schedule cache with per-season episode data, coverage and the time of the last successful API check, extending the shape compatibly. Old detail-derived snapshots display immediately but are marked `legacy` and are eligible for an API fetch at once, regardless of their detail-view timestamp. A later detail visit may seed a missing record but cannot replace checked schedule data.

For each newly checked season, replace that season's cached episodes as a set. This removes an obsolete date when an episode is rescheduled or removed from the checked season. For an unavailable season, retain its cached episodes and mark them stale; for a limited season, upsert returned episode identities while retaining unmatched cached entries, and mark the season incomplete. Match by episode ID with season/episode identity as fallback, so an updated date replaces the old date without duplication. A later complete response replaces all seasons and clears stale coverage. A partial HTTP 200 never blindly replaces a previously complete schedule.

On Home focus, read cached schedules immediately, then fetch only missing or eligible saved shows. A new show added locally or via account sync becomes eligible immediately. Complete and cap-limited responses use a twelve-hour freshness window. A temporary partial response becomes eligible after 30 minutes; an HTTP/network failure after 15 minutes. Legacy records are eligible immediately. The same in-memory per-show single-flight request and retry ledger is shared across focus and Watchlist/sync events, with at most two concurrent requests and no more than six new show requests per minute under the current production work budget. Repeated focus does not reset retry timers or start overlapping batches. On HTTP 429, pause the queue for 60 seconds and retry once; a second 429 backs off for 15 minutes. A manual Retry bypasses client freshness timers but still obeys the 429 pause and server rate limit; disable it while a request is active. An HTTP/network failure does not change the last successful check time or cached episodes. A cap-limited response is not retried early simply because its coverage is partial. On a new browser session, missing or failed entries may retry; they do not loop within a session. Schedule metadata stays device-local and outside D1 sync. Removing a show immediately removes it from Home's derived schedule; an older in-flight response must not reinsert it.

The UI derives Today, This Week and Coming Soon from one deduplicated dated episode list. Compare ISO calendar dates as dates, never by converting an air date into a timestamp. Recalculate on focus, browser/app resume, and local day change. Unknown dates are not guessed or shown in a dated section. Keep cached rows visible while requests run and incorporate each show's response as it arrives. First-time loading uses row-shaped skeletons without blocking the rest of Home.

Use provider-neutral messages in ordinary schedule UI; leave existing TMDB attribution unchanged. With no saved TV shows, show “Add TV shows to your Watchlist to see what’s coming up.” and a Search link. When every currently saved show has a sufficiently fresh complete check, Today may say “Nothing new from your shows today.”; include the next known release beneath it when available. A fully checked schedule with no upcoming dated episodes may say “No upcoming episode dates available yet.” Never show either confirmed-empty message while any saved show is missing, loading, stale or unsuccessfully checked. For incomplete coverage show “Schedule may be incomplete.” A refresh failure shows “Couldn’t update your schedule.” with Retry, while retaining cached rows. These messages may coexist when appropriate, but an error or incomplete label takes precedence over confirmed-empty copy. Do not claim that no dates have been announced.

## Verification and boundaries

Cover grouping, deduplication, rescheduling, complete-to-partial and partial-to-complete merges, watched independence, day and year boundaries, stale/error states, add/remove/sync changes, and opening a show without marking watched. Use controlled local/UAT fixtures with 30–50 saved TV shows and an empty first-load cache; exercise slow responses, partial failures, 429s, retries, repeated focus, and removal during an in-flight request. Home must remain usable and reveal each result as it arrives. A 50-show uncached load can take several minutes under the work budget; measure and report first useful row and queue completion/defer time.

Check phone-browser layouts at 360px and 390px and desktop at 1440px. Verify Home load, tab away/back, return after midnight, browser Back, direct TV-detail URL opening and reload, keyboard and expansion controls, touch targets, browser keyboard interactions, navigation near phone browser chrome, labels, and horizontal overflow. Capture phone-sized and desktop screenshots. Label browser emulation separately from actual physical-device checks; report unperformed iPhone Safari and Android Chrome checks. Run app, local API, production API and repository checks during implementation. No deployment, push or merge is authorized.

Start implementation from the web-first `feature/cloudflare-api` line in a separate worktree. The older rollback checkout has uncommitted Home changes. Recheck branch and worktree status before editing; do not overwrite either checkout. No account/auth, sync schema, D1 migration, or production release change is needed.
